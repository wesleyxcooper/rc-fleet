## Phase 2 — Custom E36 Cabriolet (3D print + aluminum panel skins)

No production lexan E36 cabriolet body exists. Design approach: printed inner structure (roll cage, chassis mounts, wheel arches, dashboard, interior) with aluminum sheet panel skins formed over printed forming bucks for flat/single-curve panels (hood, doors, roof). Compound-curve parts (overfender arches, front bumper, rear valance) print directly in TPU. Panels develop character and battle scars over time.

### Design starting point — 1:30 E36 Cabrio model scaled to 1:10

Scale the YouMagine E36 Cabrio 1994 model (1:30) by ×3 uniform in Blender to reach 1:10. The body proportions, interior, and panel geometry are correct at any scale — multiply all axes by 3.0. Supplement with a 3D scan of the physical D10 chassis (Polycam/KIRI Engine) to establish fitment geometry: wheel arch positions, floor clearance, and magnetic mount catch-point locations.

The D10 Stage launched in late September 2026. No community chassis scan exists yet. Scan your own — Polycam on an iPhone is sufficient for body post positioning and arch clearance. You don't need a perfect scan; just the rough topology.

### Clear parts — windshields and light lenses

Clear optical parts cannot be FDM or SLS printed. Three approaches:

**Option 1 — JLCPCB Transparent 8001 SLA resin (recommended):**

JLCPCB offers "Transparent 8001" as an SLA material. Customer reviews describe "glass-like transparent finish" with no layer lines. Source: [jlc3dp.com](https://jlc3dp.com). Works for windshields, headlight lenses, taillight lenses — anything that needs optical clarity or light transmission. Tolerance ±0.2mm at SLA.

After printing: sand with 1000-grit wet, then 2000-grit, then polish with Novus Plastic Polish #2 then #1. This removes the slight surface haze and achieves near-optical clarity.

For tinted lenses (tail lights, indicators): spray Tamiya transparent red or amber from the inside of the printed lens cavity after polishing — same inside-out tinting logic as lexan.

**Option 2 — Reuse Phase 1 24K light bucket accessories:**

The 24K TAF21002 (standard E36) and TAF21003 (GR Works E36) light bucket sets include the lenses. If you design the Phase 2 body to accept compatible mounting points for these accessories, you get production-quality lens geometry with zero custom work.

Trade-off: the 24K buckets are designed for the E36 M3 coupe body profile. The mounting flange geometry will need to be reverse-referenced and incorporated into the Phase 2 cabriolet design. Scan the Phase 1 buckets with Polycam, import into Blender, model the receiving recesses in the Phase 2 front bumper and rear panel to match.

This is the most practical approach for Phase 2 lights — you already own the buckets by the time you build Phase 2.

**Option 3 — Thin PETG sheet for windshield (backup only):**

0.5mm clear PETG sheet can be heat-formed over a printed buck to create a windshield. Source: any FDM filament supplier (e.g. clear PETG sheet from ePlastics, McMaster-Carr). Warp with a heat gun over a PLA/PETG buck printed to the windshield curve. Less optically clear than SLA resin but adequate if the SLA windshield fails or is delayed.

**Not suitable with FPV.** PETG sheet has enough optical distortion to degrade FPV image quality at the camera lens position. The distortion compounds over viewing distance on track. If running FPV through the windshield, use SLA Transparent 8001 only.

### Windshield and clear part mounting

Model the windshield as a distinct part in Blender — a separate piece with a perimeter flange that sits inside a channel in the body frame, like a real car windshield in its rubber gasket channel. No living hinges or snap-fits; the channel + adhesive approach is the right scale equivalent.

**Flat step + locating pins (primary approach):**

Easier to model and more forgiving than a channel. Model a flat shelf (step) around the windshield opening — one dimension to get right: step depth = windshield thickness (~1.5–2mm for SLA). The windshield sits flat on the shelf. Silicone bead around the perimeter seals it. The seam is visible from outside, but it reads as a body line.

- Step depth: match windshield part thickness (design both parts in Fusion 360 so this is parametric)
- Step width: 2–3mm inset from the exterior face — enough shelf for the silicone bead to seat

**Locating pins — modeling approach:**

Model the pins as **cylindrical protrusions directly in the body frame part file** in Fusion 360. They print as integral PA12 nylon stubs — no separate hardware. The windshield file gets the matching hole and slot as cutout geometry. Both sets of features are modeled; both print as part of their respective files.

**Why Fusion 360, not Blender:** Pins require sub-millimeter dimensional accuracy and need to stay positioned relative to the windshield opening edge. In Fusion 360, `pin_diameter = 1.5mm` is exact and the pin position is constrained to the step geometry — if the windshield opening changes, the pin re-constrains to it. In Blender you're editing mesh vertices; verifying that the pin in one file and the hole in another actually agree to 0.2mm clearance has no native tooling. You can do it in Blender (use the N panel → Item → Dimensions for exact values), but you'd be fighting the tool. Per the general workflow split: Blender for organic surfaces, Fusion 360 for anything requiring dimensional precision or cross-part consistency.

Body frame (Fusion 360): add 1.5mm diameter × 3mm tall cylinders at pin locations as parametric extrudes on the mating face.
Windshield (Fusion 360): sketch a 1.7mm circle for hole 1 and a 1.7mm × 2.5mm rounded-end slot for hole 2; cut both through the mating face.

**Why PA12 body frame gets the pins, not the SLA windshield:**

PA12 is tougher — a SLA resin pin can snap during installation and lodge in the body hole, which requires drilling out. With PA12 pins + SLA holes: replacement windshields drop onto the fixed frame pins; the body never changes.

**Use one round hole + one slot, not two round holes:**

Two round holes over-constrain the assembly: any drift between pin centers and hole centers (±0.2mm is normal for SLS/SLA) means both pins can't seat simultaneously. Source: [phorm.to alignment pins guide](https://phorm.to/blog/alignment-pins-for-3d-printed-parts/) — "Two round pins jam under 0.15mm of drift; a pin and a slot locate the part and absorb it."

Correct geometry:
- **Pin 1**: 1.5mm round pin on body frame → **1.7mm round hole** in windshield (0.2mm clearance per [makelab.com](https://help.makelab.com/help/article/how-to-design-holes-threads-and-inserts-for-3d-printing) SLA slip-fit guidance)
- **Pin 2**: 1.5mm round pin on body frame → **1.7mm × 2.5mm slot** in windshield (1.7mm wide, 2.5mm along the line between the two pins — absorbs manufacturing drift in that axis)
- Chamfer mouth of hole and slot 0.3–0.5mm × 45° so the pin self-centers on insertion

This is called exact constraint (source: [phorm.to](https://phorm.to/blog/alignment-pins-for-3d-printed-parts/), citing Blanding's *Exact Constraint*, ASME 1999).

**Channel approach (alternative — cleaner seam, more modeling work):**

If hiding the joint line matters (e.g. if the windshield seam will be visible in a photo context), add a perimeter channel instead of a flat step: 1.5mm wide × 2.5mm deep, with the windshield flange designed to match. Requires two interlocking features to agree on width and depth simultaneously. Wall thickness around the channel: 2mm minimum. Under impact, a channel transmits lateral force into the frame walls — flat step is better for a car that takes hits.

**Adhesive:**

Permatex Clear RTV Silicone (auto parts store, ~$6) is the correct material — flexible, bonds resin and plastic, sets to a rubber consistency, absorbs vibration, removable with a thin blade if needed. Apply a thin bead inside the channel before dropping the windshield in. No need for waterproof properties. Avoid CA glue (brittle, cracks under flex) and E6000 (works but is harder to remove if the windshield ever needs replacement).

**Same approach applies to side windows, rear window, and hardtop glass panels.** Light lenses are different — they're structural enough to press-fit or screw in; see Phase 1 bucket reuse above.

| Part | Approach | Mount method |
|---|---|---|
| Windshield | SLA Transparent 8001 | Channel + locating tabs + Permatex Clear RTV |
| Side windows (hardtop) | SLA Transparent 8001 | Channel + locating tabs + Clear RTV |
| Rear window (hardtop) | SLA Transparent 8001 | Channel + locating tabs + Clear RTV |
| Headlight lenses | Phase 1 24K buckets (preferred) or SLA Transparent 8001 | Press-fit recess or screw into housing |
| Taillight lenses | SLA Transparent 8001 — tint with Tamiya TS-74 inside | Press-fit recess |
| Windshield (backup) | 0.5mm PETG sheet over forming buck | Not suitable if running FPV |

### Body architecture — inner frame + swappable panel system

The inner frame is the permanent structural skeleton of the body. It mounts to the D10 chassis via the magnetic catch points, carries the roll cage, windshield frame, A-pillars, dashboard, and interior. Outer body panels (fenders, hood, doors, roof) mount to the frame at consistent attachment points and are individually removable and swappable.

**Inner frame (permanent):**
- Print in rigid PLA/ASA
- Roll cage drops into the cockpit from above, visible through the open top
- Magnetic catch points (4×) built into the floor tabs
- All panel attachment points defined here — consistent screw hole positions or clip geometry across every panel variant

**Swappable panel variants (examples for front fenders):**

| Variant | Material | Impact behavior | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock E36 arch | Aluminum sheet over forming buck | Dents, scratches, develops character | Single-curve; forming buck approach |
| E46 arch baked-in | TPU (printed) | Bounces back; paint scratches only | E46 arch profile modeled in Blender, printed as one piece, mounts to same frame points |
| Any other geometry | TPU or aluminum | Varies | Same mount interface — cross-compatible with all variants |

**Key principle:** The panel attachment interface is standardized. Every fender variant uses identical mount point positions and hardware — that's what makes them interchangeable. Model this standard in Blender before modeling any panels, then all panels reference it. Forming bucks are only needed for aluminum variants; printed variants need no buck.

**TPU panels in drift events:** TPU rebounds from barrier contact and car-to-car hits without permanent deformation. Paint on the exterior surface scratches and chips but the panel shape returns. A hard direct impact can crack thin walls, but normal drift contact will not permanently damage TPU panels.

**E46 arch widebody approach (RC analog):**
The real-car plan (E46 donor arch grafted to E36 fender via edge-to-edge weld) maps directly to the RC build — but the fabrication method changes. At 1:10 in Blender, you model the E46 arch profile directly into the fender geometry and print the widened fender as a single TPU piece. No donor fender needed; no seam to fill. The baked-in approach is the RC equivalent of the "clean fab" result you want on the real car.

## Workflow — 1:30 model → 1:10 printable modular body

1. **Scale to 1:10** — import the YouMagine E36 Cabrio 1:30 model into Blender. Scale all geometry by ×3 uniform. Verify against a known E36 dimension (wheelbase 2700mm → should read 270mm at 1:10 after scale; the D10 chassis is 260mm, so minor horizontal trim may follow).
2. **Import D10 chassis scan** — scan the physical built D10 chassis with Polycam or KIRI Engine. Import into Blender as a reference object. Position the scaled body geometry over the chassis scan to identify what needs adjustment: wheel arch positions, floor clearance, front/rear overhang.
3. **Isolate what you need** — hide or delete anything internal that won't be visible or structural: door internals, engine bay detail under hood, glass geometry. Keep: outer body panels, dashboard, seats, roll hoop/cage area.
4. **What to make from what material:**

   | Part | Material | Notes |
   |---|---|---|
   | Inner frame (floor, mount tabs, A-pillars, windshield frame) | PLA/ASA | Rigid skeleton; define panel attachment interface here first |
   | Roll cage | PLA/ASA | Drops into cockpit from above; visible through open top |
   | Interior (dash, seats) | PLA/ASA or PETG | Worth detailing — visible |
   | Hood, doors, roof panel, rear deck | Aluminum sheet over forming buck | Single-curve or near-flat panels; buck exported from Blender mesh + Solidify modifier |
   | Fender — stock arch variant | Aluminum sheet over forming buck | Same mount interface as printed variant |
   | Fender — E46 arch variant (or any other) | TPU printed | Arch baked into the geometry; bounces back from impacts; same mount interface |
   | Bumper fascia, rear valance | TPU printed | Compound curves; prints directly |
   | Hardtop (optional) | PETG or ASA | Full rigid shell; character lines modeled in Blender |
   | Flexible seals, grommets, edge trim | TPU | |

5. **Forming bucks** — for each aluminum panel, duplicate the panel surface mesh in Blender, add a Solidify modifier (~5mm backing) to make it printable, export as STL, print in PLA. Press K&S 0.016" aluminum sheet over the buck by hand or with a rubber mallet for tighter curves. Score the back of the sheet along crease lines before pressing so the aluminum folds cleanly into character lines. Trim excess with snips; file edges smooth.
6. **Attaching printed pieces to aluminum panels** — model TPU clip tabs into the printed part that hook over the aluminum edge (no fasteners, removable). For pieces that take direct impact, use M1.4–M2 machine screws into heat-set threaded inserts. Small aluminum pop rivets (1.5–2mm) are also an option and look appropriate on a drift build.
7. **3D scan for accessories** — to fit the 24K Phase 1 accessories (light buckets, exhaust, spoilers) to the custom body: spray shiny PVC parts with chalk spray first, scan with Polycam/KIRI Engine, import into Blender. Use the scan as reference for modeling recesses or mounting features in the printed structure.
8. **Magnetic catch points** — model 4 shallow recesses (10mm diameter, ~2mm deep) into the body underside at the D10 magnetic post locations. Measure from your built D10 at 260mm wheelbase before finalizing.
9. **LED integration** — model round headlight recesses directly into the front panel; rear cluster housings into the tail section. No separate buckets needed. See `magnetic-contact-design.md` for the contact interface that delivers power through the body post magnets.
10. **Interior and engine** — keep the scaled dash and seats from the 1:30 model; add/refine roll cage tubes as curves with bevel. Decorative engine visible through the hood if you model the hood as openable (TPU flexure hinge on the hood edge).

## Fusion 360 — parametric mount standards + print-ready files

Blender handles organic surface modeling. Fusion 360 handles everything that requires dimensional precision: the mount interface standard, screw holes, clip tabs, frame tabs, tolerances. All Blender geometry imports as a reference mesh — Fusion-native parametric features are built on top of it. This is the step where parts become print-ready and cross-compatible.

**Where thickness is added:**
- Body panel surfaces (TPU fenders, bumpers, cabriolet structure): apply Blender **Solidify modifier** before exporting OBJ. Fusion 360's "Thicken" command only works on B-Rep surface bodies — Blender OBJs import as mesh bodies and cannot be Thickened natively. Solidify in Blender, export as a solid mesh, Fusion receives it that way.
- Forming bucks (molds for aluminum panels): apply Solidify in Blender, export as STL, send directly to print service. No Fusion treatment needed — bucks have no precision features.
- Inner frame: model **entirely in Fusion 360**, not Blender. The inner frame is mostly geometric (rectangular tubes, flat tabs, mount bosses) — model it parametrically in Fusion from the start so the attachment standard is fully native. Import the Blender body panel meshes around it as reference.
- Clear parts (windshield, lenses): model the full solid geometry in Fusion 360 (not Blender); these require sub-millimeter accuracy for the step shelf, pins, and holes.

### D10 chassis stub — measure before starting Fusion work

Measure from the physical built D10 with calipers. Model these as a simple parametric B-Rep stub in Fusion — not a full chassis replica, just the critical interfaces.

| Measurement | How to take it |
|---|---|
| Wheelbase (axle center to axle center) | Set to 260mm; confirm after adjusting D10 |
| Magnetic post X positions (front/rear) | Calipers from front edge of chassis to post center |
| Magnetic post Y positions (left/right) | Calipers from chassis centerline to each post center |
| Post height (magnet center from chassis floor) | Calipers |
| Track width front (hub face to hub face) | Calipers |
| Track width rear (hub face to hub face) | Calipers |
| Ground clearance (chassis floor to ground) | Feeler gauge or calipers |
| Wheel outer diameter | Calipers across tire |
| Wheel arch clearance envelope | Roll wheel to full droop + full steering lock; photograph from front and side; measure extreme positions |

### Fusion 360 workflow

1. **Parameters spreadsheet first** (Modify → Change Parameters). Define the attachment standard before modeling anything:
   - `panel_mount_hole_dia = 2.2mm`
   - `panel_mount_hole_spacing` (center-to-center; choose and standardize)
   - `clip_tab_width`, `clip_tab_depth`, `clip_tab_clearance = 0.3mm`
   - `magnet_recess_dia`, `magnet_recess_depth`
   - Any other interface dimension
2. **Insert D10 chassis stub** from caliper measurements as a parametric B-Rep component.
3. **Insert Blender body panels** (File → Insert → Insert Mesh, OBJ format) as mesh body reference objects — one component per panel.
4. **Build inner frame** as a new parametric B-Rep component. Constrain it to the chassis stub. Add all panel attachment features referencing the parameters spreadsheet.
5. **Build each panel variant** as its own component. Reference the OBJ mesh for surface shape; add Fusion-native mount holes and clip features from parameters. Assembly-constrain to inner frame to verify fit.
6. **Interference check** — right-click assembly → Inspect → Interference. Resolve any clashes before exporting.
7. **Export each component as STL**: right-click component → Save As Mesh → STL. Set refinement to Fine (0.005mm chord deviation, 5° angle tolerance).

### Before sending to print service — validate STL files

- Validate each STL in **Fusion 360**: Insert → Insert Mesh. Fusion reports non-manifold geometry on import. For a dedicated repair pass: Mesh workspace → Repair → Find and Fix Issues. Alternatively, load into **PrusaSlicer** (free) — it flags non-manifold and open-surface errors before you slice.
- Check minimum wall thickness is above process minimum (FDM: 1.5mm; SLS: 0.8mm)
- Confirm part orientation in slicer preview or service preview matches intent

## Print services — no home printer

All parts ordered from a print service. **Material and process choice has a major impact on surface finish.** FDM parts have visible layer lines that require significant post-processing before painting. SLS and MJF parts have a fine matte texture that primes and paints directly, similar to model kit styrene.

| Part type | Recommended process | Material | Layer lines? | Where to order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Body panels (painted exterior) | **SLS** | PA12 nylon | None | JLCPCB, Shapeways |
| Inner frame, roll cage (structural, not painted) | FDM | ASA or PETG | Yes (internal — irrelevant) | JLCPCB |
| Flexible fenders, bumper fascias | FDM | TPU 95A | Light — sand before priming | JLCPCB (offers TPU) |
| Interior, dashboard, seats (visible, fine detail) | SLA resin | Resin | None | JLCPCB, Craftcloud |
| Forming bucks (aluminum forming tool, not final part) | FDM | PLA | Yes (irrelevant — tool only) | JLCPCB (cheapest) |

| Service | What it's good for | TPU? | SLS? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **JLCPCB** | Best price overall; FDM, SLA, SLS | ✅ TPU 95A | ✅ PA12 | Ships from China; 7–14 days. Primary service for most parts. |
| **Craftcloud** | Aggregator — quotes from multiple services | Varies | ✅ | Good for comparing prices across processes |

### Surface finish workflow — SLS body panels

1. SLS PA12 arrives with fine gritty texture — no layer lines, no support marks
2. Sand lightly with 400-grit wet/dry; wipe with IPA
3. One coat Tamiya Surface Primer — fills any remaining texture
4. Wet sand 400-grit; repeat primer if needed
5. Color coat (acrylic, enamel, or lacquer all work on primed nylon)

### Surface finish workflow — TPU fenders (FDM)

1. FDM TPU has light layer lines on outer surfaces — sand with 220-grit along layer direction, then 400-grit across
2. Tamiya Surface Primer (flexible formula — regular primer cracks on TPU)
3. Color coat
